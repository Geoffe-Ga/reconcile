# Core Entities and Concepts

This doc defines the key nouns that show up across the one-pager and the slides.

## People

### Collaborator
A **Collaborator** is a user who actively participates in writing, proposing changes, discussing, and voting.

Collaborators:
- write their own personal Mission Statements,
- join Organizations,
- and propose (“proposal”) edits to shared documents.

### Co-Creator
A **Co-Creator** is a follower/subscriber of a Collaborator, Organization, or Coalition.

Co-Creators:
- are not necessarily involved in governance,
- but receive notifications when entities they follow publish or update documents,
- and may be “pulled in” via calls-to-action.

This distinction matters because it creates an “inner loop” (collaboration + governance) and an “outer loop” (distribution + mobilization).

## Groups

### Organization
An **Organization** is any community that wants a shared living-document layer.

The source material explicitly lists examples like:
- Discord servers,
- social movements,
- Facebook groups,
- and other collective entities.

An Organization has:
- members (some subset are Collaborators),
- documents,
- votes,
- and a public-facing surface that Co-Creators can follow.

### Coalition
A **Coalition** is an umbrella entity created when two (or more) Organizations reconcile.

Key idea: a Coalition has an overarching Mission Statement that is *collaboratively created by members of the participating Organizations*.

Coalitions are the “strange bedfellows” mechanic: they make it possible to formalize overlap without requiring ideological merger.

## Content

### Document
A **Document** is any living text that the platform is used to create and revise.

Documents can be:
- personal (owned/instigated by a Collaborator),
- organizational,
- or coalition-level.

The one-pager also frames documents as a broadcast medium: after collaboration, documents can be “blasted” to Co-Creators.

### Atom
An **Atom** is the unit of change inside a document: a section, clause, paragraph, value statement, header, or other discrete piece that can be proposed and voted on.

(One of the slides literally labels the action button “Propose new [atom]”.)

Atoms exist to make editing and governance tractable:
- you can vote on a specific change,
- without re-litigating an entire manifesto.

### Proposal
A **Proposal** is a proposed change to a document atom, analogous to a pull request.

A Proposal includes:
- a title,
- a justification,
- and as much or as little proposed content as the proposer wants to include.

(Again: “Git-like,” but democratically merged.)

### Working Copy
A **Working Copy** is a temporary editable draft that a collaborator “checks out” from the main document.

Edits are automatically saved periodically.
When returning the working copy to the main file, conflicts may occur.

### Conflicts
A **Conflict** occurs when another person’s edits overlap with your edits.

The visual slides indicate:
- a side-by-side comparison is shown,
- and collaborators choose which competing text is better (then the voting process can ratify).

## Actions and Events

### Vote
A **Vote** is the governance mechanism used to accept/reject proposals (and possibly other decisions).

The slides also imply a structured voting rubric (0–4 scoring) and time-limited voting windows.

### Push
A **Push** is a notification instigated by a Collaborator (for their own docs) or by a Proposal in a larger entity.

The slides explicitly note: “TODO: Better word for ‘Push’.”
So “Push” is a working label, not sacred scripture.

### Notification
A **Notification** is any alert sent to:
- collaborators (to participate in proposals/votes),
- co-creators (to learn about newly published or updated docs),
- and potentially chat channels (to coordinate debate and time-sensitive actions).
