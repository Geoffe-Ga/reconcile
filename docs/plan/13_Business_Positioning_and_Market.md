# Business, Positioning, and Market (as implied so far)

This project’s source material is mostly “what the platform is,” not a full go-to-market plan.
Still, the language and metaphors imply a positioning strategy.

## Mental-model competitors (not direct competitors)

Reconcile is described using comparisons:

- “like opening a file in the editor screen of GitHub”
- proposals are “similar to a Pull Request”
- “blog-like activities” and broadcasting to followers (Substack-like)
- governance and voting as part of the loop

So the positioning space is an intersection of:
- collaborative writing tools,
- open-source style change management,
- community governance platforms,
- and creator/follower broadcasting.

## What makes it distinct (so far)

A few differentiators implied by the documents:

- The atomic, governable unit of text (“Atom”) as the center of political / values collaboration
- Coalition formation as a first-class workflow (not just “follow” or “partner” links)
- The explicit separation of:
  - debate space (messenger),
  - and canonical decision space (Reconcile)
- Co-Creators as an outer loop (followers who get mobilized)

## Who it seems built for (initially)

From the examples:
- Discord communities with shared causes
- social movements that need legitimacy and convergence
- community orgs that want living mission statements and plans
- politically engaged networks that want to coordinate without collapsing into purity spirals

Also: “householders” who need involvement levels and bounded cycles.

## What it might be “selling” (even if free)

Even as an open tool, Reconcile is selling:
- legitimacy (process),
- clarity (documents),
- coordination (notifications),
- and cross-group bridge-building (coalitions).

## Monetization possibilities (not decided)

Nothing in the provided material commits to monetization.
But a reader could imagine:
- free core + paid org features,
- hosted vs self-hosted,
- premium notification / coordination tools,
- verified org identities,
- moderation / safety tooling as paid layers.

Treat these as speculative until explicitly defined.

## The non-business constraint that dominates business

Because of the “database / Nazis” critique, the business story can’t ignore safety.

If the platform becomes extractive or surveillance-shaped, it fails the moral premise.
So “how it makes money” is downstream of “how it avoids becoming a panopticon.”
