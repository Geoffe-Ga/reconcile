# Philosophy and Values (as expressed so far)

Reconcile is not only a tool idea; it contains a worldview.

This doc collects the value-laden phrases and the assumptions they imply.

## “Strange bedfellows”

The platform’s headline framing is that it enables organizations to become “strange bedfellows.”

That phrase implies:
- difference is expected,
- discomfort is acceptable,
- overlap is still valuable,
- and coalition is possible without ideological assimilation.

## “Map of reconcilable ontologies”

The one-pager ends with a philosophical claim:

> In this way we create a map of reconcilable ontologies and recognize that we each agree with every other person about something and regardless of our differences, we can focus our attention and willpower on collaborating on our shared values and objectives.

That’s basically the metaphysics of the project:
- reality is plural (many frames),
- but not infinitely fragmented (overlap exists),
- and collaboration is a skill that can be scaffolded by tooling.

## The spiritual-mystical resonance (without pretending certainty)

For a spiritually mystical reader, “ontology” doesn’t just mean “belief system.”
It also means:
- what a group experiences as real,
- what it treats as sacred,
- what it fears,
- what it’s willing to do.

Reconcile’s method suggests a kind of “applied mysticism”:
- not “everyone is one” in abstract,
- but “we can locate real overlap and do something together.”

In other words: *unity through scoped collaboration, not through forced consensus narratives.*

## What is implicitly valued

From the workflows and governance:

- legitimacy (no unilateral leader deciding everything),
- transparency (proposals with justifications),
- participation gradients (involvement levels),
- bounded conflict (debate → vote → conclusion),
- institutional memory (documents hold resolved debates),
- and action orientation (documents → notifications → real-world action).

## What the philosophy does NOT guarantee

Reconcile does not magically remove:
- power dynamics,
- manipulation,
- charismatic capture,
- or bad-faith behavior.

Its philosophy is better described as:
- “design a process that makes good-faith cooperation easier”
than:
- “assume all humans will be good-faith.”

That tension becomes central in the security/threat model.
