# What is Reconcile?

Reconcile is a platform for **collaborative document editing** that creates opportunities for organizations to become “strange bedfellows” and ally themselves toward whatever objectives they genuinely share.

A clean way to picture it is: **GitHub pull requests + democratic voting + a mission-statement layer + a coalition graph**.

A weird-but-useful way to picture it is: **a civic “reality-welding” tool** for groups who are tired of being trapped in:
- infinite discourse with no consolidation,
- brittle manifestos written by one charismatic person,
- movements that fragment into splinters over wording disputes,
- and the modern internet’s tendency to reward outrage over synthesis.

Reconcile is not “a social network” in the shallow feed-first sense. The center of gravity is **documents**: living, revisable texts that represent what an individual or group stands for, what they’re doing, and what they are asking others to do.

The core promise is not utopia. It’s something more modest and more difficult: *make it easier for humans with differences to locate and maintain the overlap where coordinated action is possible.*

## The foundational one-pager (verbatim)

> Reconcile is a platform for collaborative document editing that creates opportunities for organizations to become “strange bedfellows” and ally themselves toward whatever objectives they share.
> Collaborators (users) write their own personal Mission Statements.
> They join Organizations and collaboratively create joint Mission Statements via a model for document editing resembling a democratic kind of open source Git version control.
> Organizations Reconcile into umbrella entities known as Coalitions.
> Co-Creators (followers) of Collaborators, Organizations and Coalitions receive notifications when those entities publish new or updated Documents.
> The process for Document editing is:
> View: A Collaborator (user) accesses a document in an Organization they belong to. (like opening a file in the editor screen of GitHub)
> Proposal: The Collaborator presses "Propose new Atom", edits a section and makes a Proposal (similar to a Pull Request) for changes
> Discuss: Debate in a temporary Channel, a Thread or on a Voice/Video Call, etc.
> Vote: All users of the Organization (who have opted in to participate) vote on the change
> Update: The Atom is updated (or not)
> Organizations might be anything from Discord Servers, to Social Movements, to Facebook Groups. They can Reconcile with each other to create Coalitions that then have an overarching Mission Statement that is a collaboration between both Organizations. This follows a View, Proposal, Discuss, Vote, Reconcile flow very similar to Document editing.
> A Collaborator, a Organization or a Coalition can all have Co-Creators (followers) who receive emails/pings for:
> New Documents
> Updates to Documents
> Pushes - A notification instigated by a Collaborator for their personal Documents or by Proposal (to be voted on) from a Collaborator in a larger entity.
> Votes - Organizations and Coalitions only
> The Document editing technology might be used for any number of blog-like activities, from noodling about values and philosophy, to drafting action roadmaps and outreach campaigns. It can then be blasted to all Co-Creators as a way to organize group meet-ups, disseminate voting strategy, call for help organizing, or whatever else.
> In this way we create a map of reconcilable ontologies and recognize that we each agree with every other person about something and regardless of our differences, we can focus our attention and willpower on collaborating on our shared values and objectives.

## The bet Reconcile is making

Reconcile assumes a few things (these are working assumptions, not proven laws of physics):

- Groups don’t fail only because they lack information. They fail because they can’t **converge** on shared language and shared priorities.
- “Agreement” is not binary. It can be **scoped**. Two orgs can disagree about 95% of reality and still collaborate on the other 5%.
- People will often accept governance outcomes *if the process is legible*, even when the outcome isn’t their preference.
- If you build a system that makes overlap visible, you get a practical byproduct: a **map of reconcilable ontologies**—who can work with whom, on what, and how stable that collaboration is over time.

## What Reconcile is *for* (so far)

From the source material, Reconcile is intended to support:

- drafting and maintaining personal and group mission statements,
- evolving shared values and principles over time,
- building action roadmaps, outreach campaigns, and “what we’re doing next” plans,
- broadcasting completed or updated documents to followers,
- and creating coalitions that represent *intersection missions* between groups.

## What Reconcile is *not* trying to do (yet)

Based on what’s written and diagrammed so far, Reconcile is not primarily:

- a general-purpose chat app,
- a replacement for Discord/Slack/Signal,
- a full event-management suite,
- a fundraising platform,
- or a content platform optimized for engagement metrics.

Those things may orbit the system (e.g., debates happening in a messenger app), but the “truth spine” is the document layer.
