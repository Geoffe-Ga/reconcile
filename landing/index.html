Reconcile How it works

Living documents, governed together

Find the ground you share.

Reconcile helps a group write down what it agrees on, and change it only by a vote of the people in it. Groups that disagree about most things can still act on whatever they share.

In the canopy

Every group is its own tree.

A family. A fan community. A recovery fellowship. A workplace. A movement chapter. Each keeps its own living Documents: a mission statement, house rules, points of unity.

Each group decides for itself who is in, and what its words say.

How a Document changes

Where branches meet, people propose.

  1. Propose

    A Co-Creator proposes a change to one part of a Document, with a title and a reason.

  2. Discuss

    Your group talks it over in the chat it already uses.

  3. Vote

    Voters score each version from 0 to 4 before the window closes. Competing versions share one ballot.

  4. Update

    If it passes, the Document changes, and the people who follow your group hear.

The Document isn't the last thing someone typed. It's the last edit that survived a vote.

What holds it up

Consent, not a bare majority.

A Proposal passes when its average score is at least 2.0 and fewer than 10% of voters score it 0. A change most people like still fails if too many can't live with it.

  • 0 Strongly opposed
  • 1 Opposed
  • 2 Neutral
  • 3 Supportive
  • 4 Strongly supportive

Below ground

What keeps a forest standing is out of sight.

Your group's Documents, its people and its votes live on your own devices. The relay that carries messages between them holds only sealed envelopes. It can't read them, and it can't list who is in a group.

  • No list of everyone

    There is no directory of people or groups to search or sell.

  • A separate identity for each group

    Being in one group says nothing about the others you're in.

  • Yours to undo

    Joining and following are choices you make, and you can take them back.

The network

Roots that reach each other.

Trees that look separate share water and warning through the threads between their roots. Groups can too. When two groups find something they both hold, they can reconcile: each group votes, and if both say yes they form a Coalition, with a mission statement they write together.

Each group keeps its own Documents and its own disagreements. Coalitions form by private invitation from one group to another, and there is no public map of who has joined with whom.
