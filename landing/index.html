Reconcile

verb. To find common ground, then act on it together.

Reconcile is a home for all sorts of Groups.

  • Agree by vote on Documents like your mission, values, house rules or action roadmaps.
  • Write posts, called Dispatches, for the people who follow your Groups.
  • Team up with other Groups to form Coalitions when you find common ground and a shared mission.
  • And above all, read and write the Dispatches that spread to higher-level Coalitions when the Groups composing them vote to promote them.

Dispatch A case study

How far should news of one talk travel?

A dharma teacher is giving a talk in Berkeley. He posts about it in his San Jose sangha's WhatsApp group. Here is where it goes next.

  1. San Jose sangha

    The Group votes

    They like it. They vote to share it with everyone who follows their page.

    Yes. Shared, and passed up.

  2. Bay Area Coalition

    The Coalition votes

    San Jose belongs to it, with Palo Alto and San Francisco. They want it too.

    Yes. Shared, and passed up.

  3. California Coalition

    The bigger Coalition votes

    Sanghas across the state. For Los Angeles, Berkeley is too far to drive.

    No. It stops here.

The news reached everyone close enough to come. No one else was bothered with it.

Reconcile A case study

They disagree on vaccines. They agree on microplastics.

Climate activists

A local chapter that meets on Facebook.

Holistic medicine group

A movement that talks on Reddit.

  • Vaccines. Most of one Group disagrees with the other.
  • Microplastics. Both Groups are worried.
  • A petition. It would put a proposition on the next ballot. Both would sign.

So they Reconcile

  1. Each Group votes to join up.
  2. They gather the values they share into one mission.
  3. That forms a Coalition.
  4. An action plan goes out as a Dispatch: sign the petition. Everyone in the Coalition gets it.

Neither Group has to change its mind about anything else.

Think of it as an operating system for anarcho-syndicalism: small Groups that run themselves, and decide things together by vote.

At home

Your household Discord can't agree on lights out. Your teenager Proposes a change to the house rules. Everyone talks it over and votes, right in the app.

In a movement

Your movement is too big and too mixed to write one list of demands. So members vote, and the list becomes whatever most of them agree on.

How a vote works

  • 0 Strongly opposed
  • 1 Opposed
  • 2 Neutral
  • 3 Supportive
  • 4 Strongly supportive
  • Enthusiasm counts. You score from 0 to 4, not just yes or no.
  • It passes with an average of 2 or more.
  • It's blocked if 1 in 10 voters or more give it a 0. That many people can't live with it.
Member

Collaborator

Co-Creator
Votes, writes, and Proposes changes.

Privacy Why it's built this way

A map of who believes what is worth a lot.

The risk

Governments, data brokers and advertisers would all want a tool that maps activists and their politics.

Our answer

Reconcile keeps that data where even we can't get at it.

  • Every vote and Dispatch is a sealed letter

    We pass it along. We can't open it.

  • No one outside sees who belongs to which Group

    Not us, and not anyone who asks us.

  • Not only for activists

    Families, fan clubs and sanghas get the same protection.

The network

Built so it can't hook you.

Trees that look separate are linked underground. They pass along what the others need. Reconcile is built to pass along ideas the same way, and nothing more.

  • It can't mine your attention

    We never built a way to capture it, so there is nothing to sell.

  • Advertisers can't steer you

    There are no ads, and no one can pay to push a Dispatch.

  • Influencers can't game it

    A Dispatch spreads because Groups voted for it. Never because it went viral.
